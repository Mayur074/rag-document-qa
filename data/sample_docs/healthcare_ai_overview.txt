Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare: An Overview

Introduction
Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming healthcare by enabling faster and more accurate
diagnosis, personalized treatment planning, and improved patient outcomes. Machine learning
models trained on medical imaging data can detect diseases such as pneumonia, cancer, and
diabetic retinopathy with accuracy comparable to or exceeding that of human specialists.

Key Applications

Medical Image Analysis
Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have demonstrated strong performance in analyzing
chest X-rays, MRI scans, and histopathology slides. Transfer learning using pretrained
models such as ResNet, DenseNet, and VGG has enabled high accuracy on medical datasets
even when labeled training data is limited.

Natural Language Processing in Clinical Records
NLP models extract structured information from unstructured clinical notes, discharge
summaries, and radiology reports. Named entity recognition identifies diagnoses, medications,
and procedures. Large language models assist clinicians by summarizing patient histories
and flagging potential drug interactions.

Predictive Analytics
Hospitals use predictive models to forecast patient readmission risk, ICU deterioration,
and sepsis onset. These models integrate structured EHR data, vital signs, and lab results
to generate real-time risk scores that support clinical decision-making.

Data Privacy and Ethics
Healthcare AI development requires careful handling of protected health information (PHI)
under HIPAA regulations. Federated learning enables model training across multiple
institutions without sharing raw patient data. Explainability methods such as SHAP and
Grad-CAM help clinicians understand model predictions and build trust in AI-assisted tools.

Challenges
Despite significant progress, healthcare AI faces challenges including dataset bias,
limited generalizability across institutions, regulatory approval processes, and
integration with existing clinical workflows. Addressing these challenges requires
collaboration between data scientists, clinicians, and health system administrators.
